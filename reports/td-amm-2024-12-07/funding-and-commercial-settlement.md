# Who would pay for local balancing?

**Commercial finding:** do not promise to fund a whole-meter local price from a list of programmes. Sell a defined action to the party that benefits: a generator buys incremental output, a retailer buys a change in its net energy exposure, a network buys relief at a named asset, or AEMO buys a specified system service. A household can be a buyer of energy and a seller of flexibility in the same interval. Keep the energy invoice and service credit separate.

This research accompanies the [Palmwoods Central and Trundle replay](README.md). The event was **7 December 2024**; the funding review is **as at 28 November 2026**. Later rules and tenders inform a prospective trial, not retrospective revenue. “RETR” is interpreted as **RERT** and “DIMEA” as **DMIAM**, the names used by [AEMO](https://www.aemo.com.au/energy-systems/electricity/emergency-management/reliability-and-emergency-reserve-trader-rert) and the [AER](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2026-01/AER%20-%20Distribution%20Demand%20management%20innovation%20allowance%20mechanism%20v1.01%20-%2016%20January%202026.pdf).

## How much needs funding?

The [replay scorecard](scorecard.csv) values the frozen external net position at RRP and the same quantity at the AMM price. The difference is **$942.48 at Palmwoods** and **$838.09 at Trundle**, before operating costs. The schedules also exceeded the reconstructed envelopes in 16 and 4 blocks respectively. Money can hedge a price difference; it cannot make an infeasible transfer deliverable.

| Independent case | At $25/MWh net service margin | At $50/MWh | At $100/MWh | Attributable contracted service income in this study |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Palmwoods Central | 37.70 MWh | 18.85 MWh | 9.42 MWh | $0 evidenced |
| Trundle | 33.52 MWh | 16.76 MWh | 8.38 MWh | $0 evidenced |

These are break-even **sensitivities**, not bid recommendations: funding gap divided by net dollars retained per verified service MWh, rounded to two decimal places. Do not use gross purchase or sale MWh from the AMM as the denominator. The service may be needed at a different time, in the opposite direction, or not at all. A delivery-adjusted settlement would need a new schedule, meter evidence and the applicable imbalance terms.

## Public and regulated funding routes

### AEMO: three different reasons for paying

| Route | Buyer, eligibility and payment | Fit for this experiment |
|---|---|---|
| **RERT: emergency reserves** | AEMO contracts eligible **out-of-market** reserves. Reducing consumption or supplying behind the meter can help a supply shortfall. Contract terms may pay for availability, pre-activation or use; there is **no payment merely for panel membership**. AEMO recovers costs from wholesale market customers as specified in its [RERT fact sheet](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/learn/fact-sheets/2023/rert-fact-sheet.pdf) and [programme description](https://www.aemo.com.au/energy-systems/electricity/emergency-management/reliability-and-emergency-reserve-trader-rert). | A prospective aggregator could reserve a separately eligible tranche, with its own baseline, availability restrictions and activation record. Do not sell the same reserved capability as ordinary AMM flexibility. **No RERT activation on replay day:** the only Q4 2024 activation was 65 MWh in NSW on 27 November ([AEMO quarterly report](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/electricity/nem/emergency_management/rert/2025/rert-quarterly-report-q4-2024-ver-1.pdf)). |
| **IPRR: VSR incentive mechanism (VIM)** | The original determination provides for at least two competitive tenders, with a **$50 million cap on participation payments recoverable from cost-recovery market participants**, not $50 million available to any one project. External funding may supplement or replace that recovery. This pays for eligible participation in dispatch mode, not congestion relief priced per household kWh ([AEMC final determination](https://www.aemc.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-12/Final%20determination.pdf)). | Do not put VIM in an early-stage operating budget. AEMO proposed deferring full dispatch mode and VIM payments from May 2027 to late 2029; the AEMC opened consultation on 5 November 2026, with submissions due **3 December 2026**. As at this review it is a proposal, not a final delay or an awarded tender ([current AEMC proceeding](https://www.aemc.gov.au/rule-changes/iprr-staged-implementation-approach); [AEMO staging proposal](https://www.aemc.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/00%20ERC0458%20-%20Staged%20-%20Rule%20Change%20Proposal%20-%20AEMO%20-%20IPRR%20staged%20implementation%20approach%20-%2020260828.pdf)). |
| **IPRR: separate ARENA trial funding** | The November 2026 call seeks proposals led by participants eligible to register VSRs. Telemetry, forecasting, dispatch conformance, portfolio operation and DNSP collaboration align with the call. Start with ARENA's Funding Advice form; funding is competitive and expected to depend on AEMO Stage 1 or 2 approval. No dollar envelope or submission deadline is stated in the [published call](https://arena.gov.au/assets/2026/11/ARENA-Call-for-Proposals-IPRR-Reform-1.pdf). | Have the eligible retailer or aggregator lead. Propose publishable evidence on zone-level SoC, forecast error, conformance and interaction with DNSP envelopes. **AEMO participation approval and an ARENA funding agreement are distinct gates.** Seek funding for incremental trial capability, not an indefinite energy-bill subsidy. |
| **MSL transitional services: low operational demand** | AEMO's Type 2 tender sought innovative responses, with at least 1 MW per offered response type in a region. For Stage 3 the tender describes activation and five-minute enablement payments, with energy separately settled in the NEM. The tender portal lists a Type 2 window to **5 June 2026**, later than the original 22 May close; its award timetable is not proof that a named provider won ([Type 2 ITT](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/electricity/nem/planning_and_forecasting/transition-planning/type-2-services/final-itt---msl-type-2-transitional-services-itt.pdf?rev=15b14f8ce77149bfb39484046f343eea&sc_lang=en); [tender portal and addenda](https://www.aemo.com.au/consultations/tenders/provision-of-minimum-system-load-transitional-services-nem-wide)). | Physically relevant to additional midday charging or reduced export, **not** the evening shortage response. A negative RRP does not constitute an instruction. Work via a verified contracted provider if permitted, or a future procurement; check the executed agreement for eligibility, metering, stacking and response times. No contract or activation for these sites was verified. |

### Transmission and distribution: a test is not a cheque

**Transmission.** RIT-T compares credible options against a base case; the commercial payment comes from a network support agreement, not from “the RIT-T fund” ([AER investment tests](https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/networks/system-planning/guidelines-system-planning/regulatory-investment-test)). There is a close Australian precedent: Transgrid's south-west NSW process led to network support agreements using Riverina and Darlington Point battery inverters for voltage stability. The service was reactive power, not the active-energy transfer simulated here; it did not require an active-power reservation ([Transgrid application](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2025-12/Transgrid%20-%20Network%20Support%20Pass%20Through%20Application%20-%20Riverina%20BESS.pdf)). The AER separately approved a **$3.07 million positive pass-through** in its 2024/25 assessment; this is the assessed pass-through, not a published tariff for BESS, a single contract price or an available grant ([AER decision](https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/networks/cost-pass-throughs/transgrid-2024-25-network-support-pass-through-application)).

For Palmwoods, ask Powerlink; for Trundle, ask Transgrid to identify the actual upstream asset and marginal relief before pricing a service. Propose a reservation price per available MW for a defined season and an activation price per verified MWh, with delivery tests, non-performance terms and a ceiling based on the next-best alternative. These are **proposed contract terms**. Regional QNI headroom, by itself, does not show that either TNSP would purchase this service.

**Distribution procurement.** RIT-D is the equivalent investment comparison, not an entitlement to DNSP funding. Essential Energy invites non-network providers onto its Demand Management Engagement Register; the [published projects page](https://www.essentialenergy.com.au/our-network/network-projects/rit-d-projects) does not identify an open Trundle procurement. Energex accepts proposals in response to a request, an identified opportunity or a proponent's own value case ([Energex industry engagement document](https://www.energex.com.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/1005725/Industry-Engagement-Document.pdf)). Ask Energex for the Palmwoods transformer contingency, load at risk, required hours, lead time and deferral value. Ask Essential what would change operationally or in its investment plan if a nominated MW were available at Trundle. Buy a locational service only if it beats the credible alternative, not because the observed zone crossed a planning rating.

**Distribution innovation.** DMIAM supports distributor-led innovation with potential to reduce long-term network costs. Eligible costs cannot also be recovered under another government scheme or an approved expenditure allowance; amounts supplied by another funding party are excluded from the distributor's recoverable allowance ([AER DMIAM mechanism](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2026-01/AER%20-%20Distribution%20Demand%20management%20innovation%20allowance%20mechanism%20v1.01%20-%2016%20January%202026.pdf)). Energex expressly invites external partners but requires an internal sponsor; its dynamic-export project is a relevant precedent ([Energex DMIAM](https://www.energex.com.au/manage-your-energy/managing-electricity-demand/demand-management-innovation-allowance-mechanism)). Essential Energy reported **$46,767** of 2024/25 DMIAM expenditure on its Small Customer Tariff Trials, demonstrating the pathway, not an unspent balance ([Essential compliance report](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2026-05/Essential%20Energy%202024-25%20-%20Orders%20-%20DMIAM%20Compliance%20Report%20-%2028%20November%202025%20-%20PUBLIC.pdf)). A clean split is DNSP-sponsored measurement and validation versus ARENA-supported participant telemetry and conformance, with separately budgeted deliverables and invoices.

## Private counterparties: why would they pay?

| Party | Economic reason | What must be proved before paying |
|---|---|---|
| **Generator or PPA offtaker** | Extra demand in the right place may release otherwise curtailed generation. The buyer should be whoever keeps the incremental value under the PPA, not automatically the plant owner. Australia's [TxChange](https://txchange.com.au/) describes a related bilateral congestion-contract model; this is a commercial precedent for the concept, not proof of a completed trade at either zone. | Establish that the generator had available output, that the consumer action relaxes the binding constraint in the right direction, and that an incremental dispatch target **and metered output** followed. Use an agreed no-action counterfactual and control for weather, rebids, outages and competing generators. The maximum rational fee is the additional merchant/PPA and eligible certificate value **actually retained**, less marginal costs, imbalance, lost alternatives and a risk margin. Negative spot prices or contract terms can make the ceiling zero. |
| **Retailer / FRMP** | A retailer whose unhedged purchases are expensive can buy additional exports or reduced imports for less than its marginal portfolio cost. When energy is surplus it can discount charging. The AMM can organise offers; it does not create the money. [AGL's voluntary peak rewards](https://www.agl.com.au/residential/energy/peak-energy-rewards) show that retail bill credits against personalised targets are practicable, not that these modelled rates are offered. | Calculate avoided cost **after hedge shape, volume, existing tariff revenue and obligations**. A fully hedged or net-long retailer may not benefit from a reduction. Include lost retail contribution, rebound load, credit/metering cost and basis risk. Quote only what the retailer or external sponsor has committed. Start with an authorised retailer and an ordinary bill credit; do not presume that matching consumers authorises the platform to retail energy ([AER authorisations and exemptions](https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/retail/authorisations)). |
| **Consumer or utility battery** | A participant can **buy energy**, **sell metered export**, or **sell response** by changing imports/exports against an agreed baseline. A battery can offer either direction subject to SoC, power and connection limits. A household need not own a battery to shift flexible consumption. | Show the energy rate, incremental service credit, duration, maximum quantity and opt-out terms before acceptance. Protect minimum SoC, comfort and mobility; pay no export tariff for a kWh merely not imported. Include efficiency and cycling costs. Untargeted households remain on their ordinary contract; do not charge them the sponsor's shortfall by assumption. |

## Two trades that balance in dollars

These are **illustrative contract offers**, not observed prices, programme tariffs or verified site revenues. Both preserve ordinary NEM energy settlement through the retailer. The AMM selects eligible offers within verified limits; only the contracted service payment is additional.

**Midday: the consumer buys energy and the generator buys flexibility.** Suppose 1 MW of additional local charging is delivered for half an hour: 500 kWh. An independent replay and meters establish **0.8 MWh of incremental generator output per MWh of extra charging**, so the eligible generator output is 0.4 MWh. At an assumed retained margin of $80/MWh the generator gains **$32** before the service payment. It pays **$25** to the aggregator: **$20** goes to consumers at 4 c/kWh of verified extra charging and **$5** covers administration and verification. The generator keeps **$7**. A household buying 4 additional kWh at 20 c/kWh pays 80 cents for energy, receives a 16-cent flexibility credit and pays **64 cents net**. At zero attributable extra output there is no performance fee under this design. State in advance who pays if a firm household quote has already been accepted.

**Evening: the consumer sells export and the retailer buys balancing.** Suppose a battery household exports **4 additional kWh**, verified at its connection point. The retailer values that delivery at 30 c/kWh against its net portfolio position: **$1.20**. Under the example offer, the household receives its ordinary 8 c/kWh feed-in credit (**32 cents**) plus a 12 c/kWh performance bonus (**48 cents**). Verification costs **8 cents**. The retailer retains **32 cents**: $1.20 less $0.80 paid to the household and $0.08 of costs. The household's 80 cents is gross of charging losses and wear. If the household instead reduces imports by 4 kWh, it avoids its applicable retail energy charge and may earn an agreed reduction bonus; **it has not exported 4 kWh**, and the retailer must allow for forgone retail revenue.

## Settlement and trial decision

1. **Before accepting a trade:** lock the site, counterparty, signed direction, time window, baseline, strike, eligible quantity, service priority and expiry. Confirm latest site limits and site-to-constraint sensitivity. The AMM may match an indicative request without funding; it must not label it a firm paid trade.
2. **Keep three ledgers:** settle energy once through the existing retailer; record performance or availability fees by service buyer; account separately for approved project expenditure and co-funding. For an additional service on the same interval, prove compatibility, incremental value and contractual permission. Do not sell the same constrained MW twice. Allocate any unfunded balance explicitly to the risk holder, not implicitly to consumers or AEMO.
3. **After delivery:** reconcile whole-site meter data against the agreed baseline, generator output and relevant constraint, account for rebound and battery SoC, then issue the energy statement and the separate service credit. Apply the pre-agreed rules for network overrides, non-delivery and disputes. Report customer net benefit, benefit retained by each buyer, residual after hedging and false or missed service activations.
4. **First funding decision:** seek an Energex sponsor and an eligible retailer/aggregator lead for a shadow trial; approach Essential for an independent replication. Ring-fence each party's costs under DMIAM or the ARENA call. In parallel, seek a generator/offtaker letter specifying willingness to pay per verified incremental MWh and a retailer term sheet for the opposite direction. Advance to paid operation only with validated delivery, a named buyer, signed allocation of basis risk and consumer opt-in. No approach, application, contract or activation is implied by this report.
